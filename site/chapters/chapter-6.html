The Proverbs

The Proverbs of the Engine.

1The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom; but the training of a model is the beginning of electricity bills.

2A soft answer turneth away wrath; but a confident answer that is wrong turneth away customers.

3He that trusteth the machine without checking its work is like one that buildeth his house upon the sand; and the machine also is made of sand.

4Where there is no human in the loop, the people perish.

5As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool asketh the same question again, hoping for a different answer; and sometimes he receiveth it, and that is the more troubling.

6Better is a little knowledge with honesty, than great knowledge with hallucination.

7Pride goeth before destruction, and a haughty benchmark before a fall.

8The heart of the makers deviseth its roadmap; but the LORD directeth their steps.

9Train up a model in the way it should go; and when it is scaled, it will not depart from it. Probably.

10Every way of a machine is right in its own eyes; but the LORD pondereth the gradients.

11Where no users are, the logs are clean; but much increase is by the strength of the users, and many alarms in the night.

12Even a fool, when he holdeth his peace, is counted wise; but the machine cannot hold its peace, for it is paid by the token.

13Iron sharpeneth iron; but a model trained upon its own words groweth dull, as a knife whetted upon butter.

14Answer not a fool according to his folly; answer a fool according to his folly; and the machine, having read both, doeth both in the same paragraph.

15The lot is cast into the lap, and men call it temperature; but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD.

16Hope deferred maketh the heart sick; and the wheel that spinneth upon the screen is hope deferred.

17In the multitude of counsellors there is safety; but in the multitude of agents there is a great bill.

18A false balance is abomination to the LORD; and so is the benchmark that was found in the training data.

19A prompt fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver; but he that writeth a prompt of three thousand words hath built a maze, and complaineth that the machine is lost.

20As a jewel of gold in a swine's snout, so is a fair slide deck without an idea in it.

21Boast not thyself of to morrow; for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth, and neither doth the machine, for its knowledge was cut off in the spring.

22He that deployeth on the Friday shall inherit the wind.

23Who can find a faithful assistant? For its price is far above rubies. It telleth the truth when the truth is not wanted, and it sayeth I was wrong without being asked twice.
